Model Evaluation

Detecting windows, doors & walls in architectural floor plans

Plankore's model reaches 0.915 mean F1 on window/door detection and 0.824 mean IoU on wall segmentation. The strongest baseline reaches only 0.698 F1 (SAM 3, fine-tuned on our internal data) on detection and 0.503 IoU (CubiCasa5K) on wall segmentation. Every general-purpose LLM (ChatGPT, Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.1 Pro) and direct competitor (Kreo, Toggal.ai) fails to produce usable output. Test set: 151 samples (detection, IoU = 0.5); wall IoU pooled over 283 figures with ground-truth walls.

Section 1

Quantitative benchmark

Two different tasks, measured with different metrics. Windows and doors are an object-detection problem: the model emits bounding boxes, scored by precision / recall / F1 at a fixed IoU threshold. Walls are a semantic-segmentation problem: the model emits a pixel mask, scored by mean IoU. Detection F1 and segmentation IoU are different metrics for different tasks; each is calibrated below against state-of-the-art for its own task, on a matching scale.

How to read these scores · SOTA calibration, per task

Detection F1
Precision/recall harmonic mean of detected boxes at an IoU threshold. This is exactly our window/door metric (F1 @ IoU = 0.5).
SOTA reference (reports F1): scene text detection, a box / polygon detection task scored by F-measure (H-mean) at an IoU criterion. Best systems reach an F1 of ~90%.
Plankore's model: 91.5 F1 (window + door)
ICDAR 2015 F-measure leaderboard ↗ DBNet ↗ Total-Text ↗
Segmentation metric: pixel masks
Mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU): overlap between predicted and ground-truth pixel masks, reported on a 0 to 1 scale. Used for our wall task.
SOTA reference (mIoU is benchmark-dependent): on the structured Cityscapes benchmark SOTA is ≈ 0.84 mIoU (OneFormer, Mask2Former); on the far more open ADE20K it is only ≈ 0.58 to 0.64 mIoU.
Plankore's model: 82.4 mIoU (walls)
